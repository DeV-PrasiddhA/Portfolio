DEVELOPER HUMOR

My model has trust issues. So do I.

Prasiddha Mainali · November 1, 2026 · 1 min read

LOSS:
EMOTIONALTHE CURIOSITY LOG / PRASIDDHA MAINALI

Developer humor. The dialogue below is fictional; the frustration may feel familiar.

“We need to talk,” says the validation set.

The model looks up from its training accuracy. “Can this wait? I’m doing incredibly well.”

“That’s what you said yesterday.”

“And I have a graph to prove it.”

A perfectly healthy relationship with the data

The training set is supportive. It asks familiar questions. It laughs at the same jokes. It has heard the model’s entire personality before and gives it full marks for remembering.

The validation set, meanwhile, brings a new example to dinner. Suddenly nobody knows where to put their hands.

“Have you considered,” the model asks, “that your expectations are unrealistic?”

“My expectation is that you identify a leaf.”

“In this lighting?”

The intervention

The developer opens seventeen tabs. One recommends a smaller model. Another recommends a larger model. A third suggests a groundbreaking architecture whose diagram appears to require a civil engineering degree.

The dataset sits quietly in the corner, containing three duplicate folders and a file named actually_final_labels_v7.csv.

Nobody makes eye contact with the dataset.

Developer: I think we should try regularization.
Model: I think we should stop seeing other datasets.

An unexpectedly useful ending

The joke is that it is very easy to become attached to a flattering number. A good result feels like approval; a bad one feels personal. Neither feeling helps inspect the experiment.

The useful move is to step back, check the setup, and ask what the result actually tells you. Sometimes the model needs work. Sometimes the question does. Sometimes everybody needs to read the labels.

The model has declined to comment. It is currently doing exceptionally well on a screenshot of its own training curve.
