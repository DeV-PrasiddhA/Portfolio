Dear GPU, this meeting could have been a CPU.

Prasiddha Mainali · November 1, 2026 · 1 min read

404
SLEEPTHE CURIOSITY LOG / PRASIDDHA MAINALI

A fictional letter from a piece of hardware. No GPUs were interviewed for this article.

Dear Human,

I am writing to discuss our working arrangement. When you said “one quick experiment,” I assumed we shared a definition of at least two of those words.

Regarding the phrase “just one more epoch”

You have said this fourteen times. I appreciate the optimism. I would appreciate a stopping condition more.

You also keep opening the monitoring dashboard to check whether I am working. I am working. The fan is not a decorative feature. It is my out-of-office reply.

Regarding your naming conventions

Yesterday you saved best_model.pt. Today you saved best_model_REAL.pt. I notice there is already a best_model_REAL_final_2.pt.

I cannot prove that this is the root of our problems. I am comfortable listing it as a contributing factor.

And while we are discussing communication: deleting the error message does not resolve the error. It merely makes us two coworkers who refuse to discuss the same incident.

A modest proposal

Before the next long run, please confirm that the small version works. Check a batch. Check the output. Check that the data folder is the one you intended to use. Write down what changed so tomorrow’s you does not have to conduct an archaeological expedition.

These are requests for basic project hygiene, not a demand for a sixteen-page experiment constitution.

A long run is not automatically a meaningful run. Sometimes it is a very expensive typo.

In closing

I remain excited about our mission. I support your curiosity. I even support the occasional unnecessary visualization.

I simply request that we stop describing a three-hour operation as “basically done.”

Warm regards,
Your GPU
Literally warm. Please check the vents.
