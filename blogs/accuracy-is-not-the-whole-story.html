Your model scored 99%. Now ask the awkward questions.

Prasiddha Mainali · November 1, 2026 · 1 min read

99%
…OF WHAT?THE CURIOSITY LOG / PRASIDDHA MAINALI

A score is an answer to a particular question. Without the question, “99%” is mostly excellent typography.

Before designing a celebratory project card, write down what was measured, on which data, and how that data was selected. If this explanation is harder to produce than the number, the next task is probably documentation.

Was the test really separate?

Training and evaluating on the same examples can produce misleadingly strong results. Keep test data separate, and avoid repeatedly tuning your choices against it. Use a validation set or an appropriate cross-validation procedure for model selection, reserving the test set for final evaluation.

Preprocessing also matters: transformations learned from data should be fitted on training data and applied to held-out data. The scikit-learn cross-validation guide explains these distinctions and shows how pipelines help.

What would make the number useful?

Imagine showing the result to a teammate who has never opened your notebook. They should be able to understand the task, the comparison, and the limitations without interviewing you.

A useful results note can be short:

  • What decision is this experiment meant to inform?
  • Which data and evaluation procedure produced the score?
  • What simpler approach are we comparing against?
  • Which mistakes deserve a closer look?
  • What remains untested?

Show the uncomfortable examples

A polished demo naturally invites the examples that look best. A useful investigation also makes room for the confusing ones. Collect them. Describe them without immediately inventing an explanation. Treat them as questions for the next experiment.

The point is not to make every project sound uncertain. It is to make its confidence understandable.

The most convincing result is one another person can question—and still understand.

Keep the large number if it helps communicate. Just give it enough context to earn its font size.
